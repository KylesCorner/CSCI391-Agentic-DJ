# Music glossary — original Encore educational definitions

These definitions are for retrieval context and are not claims that individual recordings have these properties.

## Swing
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A jazz rhythmic approach with a characteristic long-short subdivision and forward-driving pulse, prominent in big-band music.

## Big Band
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A large jazz ensemble typically organized around saxophone, trumpet, trombone, and rhythm sections.

## Gypsy Jazz
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A style associated with Django Reinhardt and the Hot Club tradition, often centered on acoustic strings and lively swing rhythm.

## Bebop
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A jazz style emphasizing fast tempos, complex harmonic lines, virtuosic improvisation, and small combos.

## Cool Jazz
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A jazz approach associated with relaxed phrasing, restrained dynamics, and often carefully arranged ensemble textures.

## Hard Bop
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A jazz style integrating bebop vocabulary with blues, gospel, and strongly accentuated rhythms.

## Free Jazz
Source: Encore-authored glossary
An improvisational jazz approach that may loosen predetermined harmony, meter, or formal constraints.

## Ragtime
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A piano-centered style known for syncopated melody against a steady accompaniment, influential on early jazz.

## Dixieland
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A traditional-jazz style using collective improvisation and early New Orleans ensemble instrumentation.

## New Orleans Jazz
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Early jazz from the New Orleans tradition with ensemble interplay, brass and reed leads, and rhythmic syncopation.

## Latin Jazz
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Jazz combining improvisational vocabulary with Latin American rhythmic patterns and instrumentation.

## Bossa Nova
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Brazilian song and rhythm style featuring understated syncopation, gently flowing pulse, and characteristic guitar figures.

## Samba
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A family of Brazilian rhythms and musical practices often characterized by layered percussion and a buoyant pulse.

## Blues
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A broad musical tradition often built around expressive phrasing, blue notes, and recurring harmonic forms.

## Fusion
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Music combining jazz improvisation with rock, funk, electronic, or other contemporary styles.

## Funk
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Groove-focused music with interlocking syncopated rhythms, strong bass lines, and emphasis on the downbeat.

## Soul
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Popular music blending influences such as gospel, rhythm and blues, and expressive vocal performance.

## Ambient
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Music emphasizing soundscape, atmosphere, and sustained texture over prominent song development.

## Downtempo
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A broad electronic or beat-oriented category with relatively relaxed tempos and spacious arrangements.

## Lo-Fi
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A production aesthetic with audible imperfections or textured, intentionally less polished sound.

## Shoegaze
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Rock with dense layered guitars, effects, blended vocals, and immersive atmospheric textures.

## Post-Rock
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Rock-oriented instrumental or experimental music emphasizing evolving texture and extended structures.

## Instrumental
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Music presented without sung lyrics as its principal content; the word in a recording title alone is insufficient proof.

## Acoustic
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Music centered on non-electrically generated instrument sounds, though modern recordings may still use electronic production.

## Upbeat
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A mood or energy descriptor suggesting lively, positive, animated music; not a standardized genre.

## Mellow
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A mood descriptor suggesting soft intensity, smoother textures, and relatively subdued dynamics.

## Focus Music
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A functional listening label for music intended to support concentration, often with low distraction; subjective rather than a formal genre.

## Study Music
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A context label for music chosen while studying; preferences about vocals, tempo, and familiarity differ across listeners.

## B-Side
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Historically the reverse side of a vinyl single, sometimes used informally for a less-promoted recording.

## Ep
Source: Encore-authored glossary
An extended-play release, typically longer than a single but shorter than a full-length album; platform classification varies.

## Lp
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A long-playing record or album-format release, historically associated with the 33⅓ RPM vinyl format.

## Remix
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A reworked version of a recording that changes elements such as arrangement, structure, or production.

## Live Recording
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A performance recording captured in a live setting, which may differ markedly from its studio counterpart.

## Cover Version
Source: Encore-authored glossary
A newly performed recording of a song associated with an earlier performer or recording.

## Scrobbler
Source: Encore-authored glossary
Software that reports listening events to a tracking service such as ListenBrainz.
